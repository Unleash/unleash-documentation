## Overview

The [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/29/the-nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20/final) (CSF) 2.0 is a voluntary framework that many security teams use to organize their controls and assess vendors. The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) (AI RMF 1.0) plays the same role for AI systems. Neither is a certification: an organization aligns its own program to the framework and asks suppliers to show which outcomes their products support.

A feature management platform touches a specific set of CSF outcomes: change control for production applications, the identities and permissions that decide who can change production behavior, log records, and the fastest way to contain a faulty feature. For AI features, it is the mechanism the AI RMF asks for to supersede, disengage, or deactivate a system that is not behaving as intended.

This guide outlines how [Unleash Enterprise](https://www.getunleash.io/pricing) features align with those outcomes. For a summary of all frameworks, see the [compliance overview](/privacy-and-compliance/compliance-overview). For the NIST SP 800-53 control families, see [FedRAMP compliance](/privacy-and-compliance/fedramp).

## Which documents this page covers

The following table lists the NIST documents that the mappings on this page cite.

<StickyTable>
| Document | What it is |
|----------|------------|
| NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 (NIST CSWP 29) | Six functions (Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover) broken into categories and subcategories. The subcategory IDs cited on this page, such as PR.PS-01, are the outcomes an organization maps its controls to. Published February 26, 2024, replacing CSF 1.1. |
| NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (NIST AI 100-1) | Four functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) with numbered subcategories, such as MANAGE 2.4, for managing risk across the AI lifecycle. Published January 26, 2023. NIST is revising the AI RMF as part of the July 2025 AI Action Plan; this page maps to AI RMF 1.0. |
| Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (NIST AI 600-1) | A companion to the AI RMF that lists generative AI risks and suggested actions, keyed to AI RMF subcategories. Published July 26, 2024. |
</StickyTable>

The provider does not manage feature flags; flag changes go through change requests instead. The [ServiceNow integration](/integrate/servicenow) mirrors each change request into ServiceNow as a standard change. |

Unleash also supports fully air-gapped deployments. |

## How Unleash features map to the AI RMF

The AI RMF asks organizations to govern, map, measure, and manage AI risk across the lifecycle. Feature flags sit at the deployment and operation end of that lifecycle: they control when an AI system starts serving users, to whom, and how you switch it off.

Unleash provides controls and evidence for the runtime configuration of an AI system. You meet the rest of each outcome through your own processes and the other tools in your AI stack.

<StickyTable>
| Outcome | Description | Unleash feature |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| AI system inventory and decommissioning: GOVERN 1.6, GOVERN 1.7 | Mechanisms are in place to inventory AI systems, and processes exist for decommissioning and phasing out AI systems safely. | When you put each AI feature behind a [flag](/concepts/feature-flags), it gets an inventory entry with an owning project, a type, and a lifecycle stage, and disabling and archiving the flag is a recorded decommissioning step. [Technical debt](/concepts/technical-debt) reporting shows flags that have passed their expected lifetime, so AI features that are due for retirement surface for review. |
| Roles, accountability, and human oversight: GOVERN 2.1, MAP 3.5 | Roles and responsibilities for AI risk are documented, and processes for human oversight are defined, assessed, and documented. | [Change requests](/concepts/change-requests) make a named person the approver of any change to how an AI feature is exposed, and [custom roles](/concepts/rbac) restrict who can approve or apply. AI agents that change configuration act through [service accounts](/concepts/service-accounts) with scoped permissions and go through the same approvals, so the [event log](/concepts/events#event-log) shows whether a person or an agent made each change. |
| Monitoring in production: MEASURE 2.4, MEASURE 2.6, MEASURE 3.1 | AI system functionality and behavior are monitored in production, safety metrics reflect real-time monitoring and response times for failures, and emergent risks are tracked in deployment. | [Impact metrics](/concepts/impact-metrics) tie error rates, latency, and business outcomes to the flag that controls the AI feature, and release plan safeguards pause a rollout automatically when a threshold is crossed. [Signals](/concepts/signals) accept alerts from your model monitoring stack. |
| Supersede, disengage, or deactivate: MANAGE 2.4 | Mechanisms are in place and applied to supersede, disengage, or deactivate AI systems that demonstrate performance or outcomes inconsistent with intended use. | Disabling the flag deactivates the AI feature for all users in seconds, or for a specific [segment](/concepts/segments), without a deployment. [Strategy variants](/concepts/strategy-variants) supersede one model version with another, or fall back to a non-AI path, by routing a defined share of traffic. [Actions](/concepts/actions) can disable the flag automatically from a monitoring signal. |
| Post-deployment monitoring, change management, and incident response: MANAGE 4.1, MANAGE 4.3 | Post-deployment monitoring plans cover appeal and override, decommissioning, incident response, recovery, and change management, and incidents and errors are communicated to relevant parties. | Change requests, release templates, and the event log give AI features the same change management and incident record as any other production change. Staged exposure through release templates, starting with an internal allow-list, lets a team observe an AI feature before customers see it. |
| Generative AI deployment and incident actions: AI 600-1, suggested actions under MANAGE 1.3, MANAGE 2.4, and MANAGE 4.1 | The profile's suggested actions for generative AI include considering a staged release approach, procedures for disengaging or deactivating a system and escalating incidents, and post-deployment monitoring. | You can deploy every generative AI feature behind a flag with a kill switch, expose it first to an internal allow-list, expand it by percentage or segment, and withdraw it in seconds, with impact metrics recording the result at each stage. |
</StickyTable>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Is Unleash NIST certified?">
No. NIST does not certify any product against the CSF or the AI RMF; they are voluntary frameworks that an organization applies to its own program. A security review can check which CSF outcomes a vendor's controls support, which is what this page lists.

Unleash holds a SOC 2 Type II attestation and provides control mappings for [ISO 27001](/privacy-and-compliance/iso27001) and [FedRAMP](/privacy-and-compliance/fedramp). It does not hold an ISO 27001 certificate or a FedRAMP authorization.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does this page cover NIST SP 800-53?">
Not directly. SP 800-53 Rev. 5 is the control catalog behind FedRAMP, and [FedRAMP compliance](/privacy-and-compliance/fedramp) maps Unleash features to its control families: access control, audit and accountability, security assessment and authorization, configuration management, identification and authentication, system and communications protection, and system and information integrity.

NIST publishes Informative References online that map CSF 2.0 subcategories to SP 800-53 controls, so you can read the two pages together.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do the AI RMF and the EU AI Act relate?">
The AI RMF is a voluntary US framework; the EU AI Act is binding law for AI systems placed on the EU market. They overlap on human oversight, logging, post-market monitoring, and the ability to intervene in or stop a system. [EU AI Act compliance](/privacy-and-compliance/eu-ai-act) maps the same Unleash features to the Act's articles.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does the AI RMF apply to AI agents that change configuration?">
Yes. The AI RMF applies to any AI system, and the Generative AI Profile names autonomous agents among the security threats to assess. In Unleash, you give an agent its own [service account](/concepts/service-accounts) with scoped permissions, its changes go through [change requests](/concepts/change-requests) like a human user's, and the [event log](/concepts/events#event-log) attributes each change to the agent's identity. This is the oversight and audit record that the Govern and Manage functions ask for.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Unleash support the four-eyes principle for production changes?">
Yes. [Change requests](/concepts/change-requests) enforce review and approval before a change is applied to a protected environment. You can require between 1 and 10 approvers, restrict who can approve through custom roles, and require approval for flag changes, strategy changes, and segment changes. Users with the Admin root role can approve and apply their own changes, so limit that role to keep the four-eyes principle in place. Unleash retains the full history of each change request in the [event log](/concepts/events).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which Unleash plan do I need?">
The controls that map most directly to CSF 2.0 and AI RMF outcomes are [Enterprise features](/support/oss-comparison): change requests and approval workflows, custom roles and environment-level permissions, SSO and SCIM, more than two environments, release templates, impact metrics with safeguards, signals and actions, login history, and Enterprise Edge. The event log, segments, strategy constraints, and the open-source SDKs are available in all editions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where can I get the documents my assessment requires?">
The Trust Center provides the SOC 2 Type II report, penetration test summaries, business continuity and disaster recovery policies, and security policies on request. For subcontractor lists, hosting locations, and contractual terms, [contact us](https://www.getunleash.io/plans/enterprise).
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
