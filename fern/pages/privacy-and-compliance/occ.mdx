## Overview

The [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency](https://www.occ.gov) (OCC) supervises national banks, federal savings associations, and federal branches and agencies of foreign banks. There is no single OCC regulation for information and communication technology risk. Examiners assess technology, change, and vendor controls against regulations, interagency guidance, and the Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council (FFIEC) IT Examination Handbook. The OCC issues most of these documents jointly with the Federal Reserve and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), so the same expectations apply to state banks and bank holding companies.

Across these documents, examiners look for the same things: changes are approved, tested, attributed, and reversible; access follows least privilege and segregation of duties; third-party software is assessed and monitored; and incidents are detected, contained, and reported. A feature management platform is in scope because it controls application behavior in production.

This guide outlines how [Unleash Enterprise](https://www.getunleash.io/pricing) features align with those expectations. For a summary of all frameworks, see the [compliance overview](/privacy-and-compliance/compliance-overview).

## Which guidance this page covers

The following table lists the documents that the mappings on this page cite and the institutions each one applies to. Supervisory guidance is not enforceable in itself, but examiners use it to assess safety and soundness, and findings under it are cited through 12 CFR Part 30.

<StickyTable>
| Document | Applies to |
|----------|------------|
| Interagency Guidelines Establishing Information Security Standards (12 CFR 30, Appendix B) | All OCC-supervised institutions, including federal branches and agencies of foreign banks. |
| Computer-Security Incident Notification Rule (12 CFR Part 53) | All OCC-supervised banking organizations and their bank service providers. |
| Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (OCC Bulletin 2023-17) | All OCC-supervised institutions. |
| FFIEC IT Examination Handbook: Architecture, Infrastructure, and Operations (2021), Development, Acquisition, and Maintenance (2024, OCC Bulletin 2024-26), Information Security, and Business Continuity Management booklets | Examiner procedures for all OCC-supervised institutions, including federal branches and agencies. |
| Interagency Paper on Sound Practices to Strengthen Operational Resilience (OCC Bulletin 2020-94, SR 20-24) | Firms with \$250 billion or more in assets, or \$100 billion or more with specified risk indicators. |
| OCC Heightened Standards (12 CFR 30, Appendix D) | Covered banks with \$50 billion or more in average total consolidated assets. |
| Model Risk Management: Revised Guidance (OCC Bulletin 2026-13, SR 26-2, FDIC FIL-15-2026) | All OCC-supervised banks; most relevant to banks with more than \$30 billion in total assets. |
</StickyTable>

## How Unleash features map to OCC expectations

Each of the following tables cites the document and the specific expectation, then describes the Unleash feature that supports it.

Unleash provides controls and evidence for the parts of each expectation that concern the feature management platform itself. You meet the rest through your own risk management program.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Is Unleash certified or approved by the OCC?">
No. The OCC supervises banks, not software products, and it does not certify vendors. The OCC expects your bank to assess Unleash under its third-party risk management process and to show examiners the controls and evidence described on this page.

Unleash holds a SOC 2 Type II attestation and provides control mappings for [ISO 27001](/privacy-and-compliance/iso27001) and [FedRAMP](/privacy-and-compliance/fedramp). It does not hold an ISO 27001 certificate or a FedRAMP authorization.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is Unleash examined by the OCC as a bank service provider?">
No. Under the Bank Service Company Act, the agencies may examine companies that provide services to banks, and they do so for a small number of significant providers such as core processors. Unleash has not been designated or examined.

Whether hosted Unleash is a bank service provider for the purposes of the incident notification rule in 12 CFR Part 53 depends on how your bank uses it. Enterprise agreements set out Unleash's incident notification commitments either way. Self-hosted Unleash is software running in your own environment, and examiners review it as part of your own systems.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does this apply to the US branch of a foreign bank?">
Yes. The OCC licenses and examines federal branches and agencies of foreign banks under the same safety and soundness framework, and OCC bulletins, including the third-party guidance and the FFIEC handbook releases, are addressed to them. Examiners generally allow a branch to rely on head office systems and policies, but they expect the branch to show that those controls operate for the branch's own activities and that it can produce the evidence locally.

Because you can scope flags by environment, project, and segment, a branch can run on a group-wide Unleash instance while keeping its own approvals, roles, and audit trail for the features it exposes to US customers.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does OCC guidance apply differently to hosted and self-hosted Unleash?">
Yes. [Hosted Unleash](https://www.getunleash.io/pricing) is a third-party relationship, so it falls under the third-party guidance: due diligence, contract provisions, ongoing monitoring, and termination planning, plus service provider oversight under 12 CFR 30, Appendix B.

Self-hosted Unleash runs inside your own infrastructure. It's still software you license and should assess under supply chain risk management, but the operational controls, such as hosting, encryption, backups, network security, and log retention, are yours. This is why many banks choose to self-host.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What will change if the 2026 third-party risk management proposal is finalized?">
The proposed guidance would replace OCC Bulletin 2023-17 with a shorter, principles-based document organized around four components: identify and assess risk, oversee risk proportionately, make residual risk decisions, and establish governance. It keeps the same lifecycle stages (due diligence, contracting, ongoing monitoring, and termination) but scales the depth of each to the assessed risk of the relationship rather than the type of activity.

The mappings on this page are written against those stages, so they remain valid under either version. Unleash will update this page when the agencies issue the final guidance.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Unleash support the four-eyes principle for production changes?">
Yes. [Change requests](/concepts/change-requests) enforce review and approval before a change is applied to a protected environment. You can require between 1 and 10 approvers, restrict who can approve through custom roles, and require approval for flag changes, strategy changes, and segment changes. Users with the Admin root role can approve and apply their own changes, so limit that role to keep the four-eyes principle in place. Unleash retains the full history of each change request in the [event log](/concepts/events).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can Unleash stay available during an outage of the Unleash API or the network?">
Yes. Once initialized, [SDKs](/sdks) keep their flag configuration in a local cache and continue evaluating flags during an outage; they only stop receiving updates. At startup, an SDK needs to reach Unleash or Edge unless it supports and is configured for [bootstrapping](/sdks#bootstrap); without flag data, flags evaluate as disabled or to the default value defined in code. [Unleash Enterprise Edge](/unleash-edge) adds a resilient caching layer that continues to serve evaluations and collect metrics while the API is unreachable. Unleash also supports fully air-gapped deployments.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Unleash process customer information covered by 12 CFR 30, Appendix B?">
With backend SDKs and Unleash Edge, flags are evaluated locally and end-user data does not need to be sent to the central Unleash service. Frontend SDKs that call the Frontend API directly send evaluation context to the endpoint they connect to, which can be [Unleash Enterprise Edge](/unleash-edge) inside your own infrastructure. With a self-hosted Unleash instance, no end-user data leaves your infrastructure at all. For more details, see [Data collection and privacy](/privacy-and-compliance/data-privacy).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which Unleash plan do I need?">
The controls that map most directly to OCC expectations are [Enterprise features](/support/oss-comparison): change requests and approval workflows, custom roles and environment-level permissions, SSO and SCIM, more than two environments, release templates, impact metrics with safeguards, signals and actions, login history, and Enterprise Edge. The event log, segments, strategy constraints, and the open-source SDKs are available in all editions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where can I get the documents my vendor assessment requires?">
The Trust Center provides the SOC 2 Type II report, penetration test summaries, business continuity and disaster recovery policies, and security policies on request. For subcontractor lists, hosting locations, and contractual terms, [contact us](https://www.getunleash.io/plans/enterprise).
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
